# Photo shoot brief

Compiled 20/08/2026. What to shoot, where, and why each one earns its place on the site.

This document exists because the suburb pages need local proof and Flowtek does not have
it yet. The honest way to get it is to photograph it. Not to composite it, not to generate
it, and not to borrow another business's photograph. `/OS/brand/photography.md` bans a
generated tradesperson by name, and every suburb page currently carries a caption saying we
have not recorded which street a job was on. That caption has to stay true.

---

## Part 2. The service shots

Six services, six photographs. These replace the current arrangement, where a suburb page
shows a job photo matched by service type with a caption admitting we do not know where it
was taken.

**The method is already written.** `photography.md` has a four frame shot list per job:
wide for context, mid of the tradesperson working, tight on the finished connection, and the
job left clean. It takes two minutes. Do it on jobs you are already on and this section
fills itself in a month.

| Service | The shot that sells it | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| Hot water | Mid shot, hands on the unit, old unit visible on the ground beside the new one | The before and after in a single frame. Nothing explains the job faster |
| Blocked drains | Tight on the camera screen or the rods going in, with the gully in frame | Shows equipment a handyman does not own. The current riser photo does this well already |
| Renovations | Rough-in on an open wall, pipework exposed before it is closed up | The whole argument of the renovation page is "the part you cannot see". Show it |
| Gas fitting | Tight on a compliant connection with the test point visible | Gas is the licensed line. Photograph the thing that proves it was done properly |
| Backflow and TMV | Hands on the device with the test kit connected | The only service that recurs on a calendar. A commercial customer needs to see the test happening |
| Emergency | Water off at the meter, or the make safe before the repair | Not the drama, the control. What a customer wants to see is someone taking charge |

**One rule for all six.** Charcoal polo with the orange collar, per the uniform of record.
The old royal blue tee shot is a real Flowtek photo but the shirt is off brand, so it has
been kept off every hero slot on this site pending brand register row 16.

## Part 3. The suburb shots

This is the part that matters most, and the part that has to be real.

### Why the van is the answer

Jason drives to these suburbs anyway. The van is:

- **His.** No copyright question, no borrowed photograph.
- **Instantly recognisable.** Orange and navy, wordmark the width of the panel.
- **Proof of presence.** The van parked at a place a local knows is evidence he was there,
  because he was.
- **Repeatable.** One photo per suburb, taken on a job he was already going to.

Park where the landmark is behind or beside the van, get the whole vehicle in frame, and
shoot in landscape. Thirty seconds per suburb.

### If Jason wants to be in the shot

Better than the van alone, and still honest, as long as it actually happened:

- Jason beside the van at the landmark. One frame, thirty seconds, and it is true.
- Jason on a real job in that suburb, with anything that identifies the suburb in the
  background. This is the strongest possible version and it costs nothing but remembering.

### What must not happen

- **No customer's face, house number, street sign or number plate without asking.**
  `photography.md`, permission and privacy. If consent is unclear, shoot tight on the work.
- **No photographing members of the public** to stand in for "the community". A stranger in
  a shopping centre car park has not agreed to appear in a plumber's advertising.
- **No borrowing a photograph** of a landmark from the web. Those are somebody's copyright.
- **No composite and no generated image.** A photograph of Jason somewhere he has not been
  is a false representation about the business, and on a page headed "Plumber in Emu Park"
  it is read as evidence of local presence. It is also the one thing
  `photography.md` bans by name.

## Part 4. Technical minimums

Straight from `photography.md`, so nobody has to remember two sets of rules.

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Resolution | 2016 px minimum on the long edge. A current phone easily clears this |
| Orientation | Landscape for the site. **Rotate upright before sending.** Four photos so far have arrived sideways |
| Lens | Clean it first. Do not use digital zoom, step closer |
| Processing | No filters, no presets, no watermark. Straighten and crop only |
| Naming | `suburb-service-YYYYMMDD.jpg`, for example `emu-park-hotwater-20260822.jpg` |
| Uniform | Charcoal polo, orange collar, tan boots, tool belt on the hips |

**Send them however is easiest.** The naming matters more than the delivery method, because
the file name is the only record of which suburb a photograph belongs to, and that is
exactly the information the current library is missing.

## Part 5. How each photograph reaches the site

Two steps, and the second one is one line.

1. Drop the file in `src/assets/`.
2. Add it to the `photoSets` map in `src/pages/plumber-[suburb].astro`, or to a new
   `suburbPhoto` field on that suburb in `src/data/suburbs.js`.

The moment a real suburb photograph exists, the honest caption on that page
("we have not recorded which street it was on") comes off and is replaced by the suburb
name. That is the caption changing because the facts changed, which is the only reason a
caption should ever change.
